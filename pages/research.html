Research

Research Themes

Language-in-Education Policy

I study how language policies are written, mediated through institutions, and enacted — or resisted — in classrooms. My work documents the gap between official policy and lived practice in Senegalese secondary and higher education, drawing on macro–meso–micro analytical frameworks and institutional ethnography.

Translanguaging & Decolonial Pedagogy

I apply Ubuntu Translanguaging Theory and the Sankofa Principle to theorize and document how multilingual learners and teachers draw on their full linguistic repertoires as a resource rather than a deficit. This work intersects with decolonial critiques of monolingual schooling in postcolonial contexts.

Critical & Multimodal Discourse Analysis

I use critical discourse analysis and multimodal methods to examine how political actors, media institutions, and education policy texts construct and contest ideological positions. Current projects analyze the Senegalese press and state broadcasting as sites of ideological struggle.

Frameworks & Instruments

Translanguaging Classroom Experience Scale (TCES) Instrument under development
A 24-item, six-domain scale measuring Senegalese secondary students' experiences of translanguaging pedagogy. Theoretically anchored in Ubuntu Translanguaging Theory, Sociocultural Theory, Community Cultural Wealth, Decolonial Theory, and Critical Language Awareness. The 41-page instrument manual outlines nine validation phases. The TCES has not yet been validated.
Integrated Multi-Level Qualitative Analytical Framework (IMQAF)
Developed for the UCAD dissertation. Combines macro (national policy), meso (institutional mediation), and micro (classroom enactment) levels to trace how CLT policy is interpreted and enacted across Senegalese secondary schools. Situated within the macro–meso–micro tradition of language policy research (Kaplan & Baldauf, 2003; Spolsky, 2009).

Critical AI Literacy

A developing research direction — documented activities only. No claim of established AI expertise, funded project, or technical programming skills.

Critical AI literacy extends my work on multilingual and decolonial pedagogies to questions of generative AI in education. It draws on the same analytical questions I bring to language-in-education policy: who determines what counts as knowledge, in whose language, and through whose tools. The four activities below represent the documented scope of this work to date.
